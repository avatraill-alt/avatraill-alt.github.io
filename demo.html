1. What is an informed user?

An informed user is someone who stays current with information systems and information technology. The textbook identifies six benefits of being an informed user. One important benefit is that informed users are valuable because, as discussed in class and stated in the textbook, “they obtain greater value from any technologies they use.” Informed users can also improve system quality by providing meaningful input about a company’s IT applications, which can strengthen the overall effectiveness of those systems. Another benefit is that informed users are better prepared to avoid or address software glitches because they understand what to watch for and how to respond to potential problems. Staying current is also important. As the textbook explains, “Being an informed user will keep you abreast of both new information technologies and rapid developments in existing technologies.” In addition, informed users are better able to participate in discussions about technology with coworkers, friends, and family.

2. Do you want to be an informed user?

Yes, I want to be an informed user because knowledge of information systems and information technology can make me a more valuable employee. Even if my future career does not directly involve IT, understanding these systems will still be useful in the workplace. I plan to pursue a career in Human Resources, and section 1.2 of the textbook explains that an HR department may use one application to screen job candidates and another to track employee turnover. If these applications experienced IT problems and I did not understand the issue, I would need to wait for the IT department to resolve it. However, if I had enough knowledge to identify or fix the problem myself, I could help the company avoid delays in the hiring process while also demonstrating initiative and competence.

3. What does “constant call” mean?

Constant call means being available 24/7. This means you're available to your employers and clients at any time. This does not mean that you will get called every day or night, but you may get calls during vacations.

4. Do you like being on constant call?

When I first read the chapter, I was against it. I thought to myself, “Why would I want to be available 24/7 when I’m not getting paid for it?” However, in class, Professor Wagner shared his own experience and explained why being on constant call is good and now common in the business world, not just in IS and IT. His discussion of constant call made me change my mind. Being on call 24/7 is not as horrible as I originally thought. It shows your employers and clients that you are invested in your job and willing to help when needed. Showing you are willing to put in the work and hours makes you an invaluable employee, which may increase your chances of getting promotions.

5. Discuss from the view of your employer.

From an employer’s perspective, having employees on constant call can be beneficial because it ensures that someone is available when urgent client or business needs arise. This availability can help prevent delays, maintain strong client relationships, and support business operations across different time zones. For example, my father was on constant call early in his career. He sometimes woke up at midnight to attend virtual board meetings in China. Growing up around this experience helped me understand that being on constant call can be demanding, but it can also provide clear benefits for employers who need timely communication and flexibility.

6. The perspective of an employee.

From an employee’s perspective, being on constant call can be tiring and may strain personal relationships if family members and loved ones do not understand why the responsibility matters. I found a study from the National Library of Medicine that discusses work-life balance. Although the study does not specifically use the term “constant call,” it is relevant when considering the possible disadvantages of always being available for work. The study explains that health is critical to achieving work-life balance and notes that participants rated health and family as equally important. If an employee is constantly on call, they may spend much of their personal time thinking about when they will be needed and what the situation might involve. This can make it difficult to mentally disconnect from work and rest. The study also shows that work-health balance strongly influences job satisfaction.
